A good **LIC plan presentation** is often the difference between "I'll think about it" and "where do I sign?" Clients rarely say no to a plan because the plan is bad. They say no because they didn't understand what they would pay, what they would get, and why it suits *their* family.

This guide shows LIC agents how to build a clear, honest presentation in under ten minutes. It covers a proven structure, Hindi phrases that land well with clients, and free tools for producing a WhatsApp-ready PDF.

## Why most LIC presentations fail

Agents often share a dense screenshot full of numbers: tabular premium, SRB, FAB, mode rebate. To a client, it looks like a bank statement in a foreign language. Common problems include:

- **Too many plans at once.** Five options create confusion, and confusion means "baad mein dekhte hain" (let's see later).
- **No link to the client's goal.** The numbers don't connect to their daughter's college fees or their retirement.
- **Guaranteed and non-guaranteed figures mixed together.** It looks like overpromising, even when it isn't intended.
- **No clear next step.**

## The 6-part structure that works

### 1. The client's goal, in their words

Open with one line: "Aapka goal: Riya ki higher education ke liye 2040 tak ₹25 lakh" ("Your goal: ₹25 lakh for Riya's higher education by 2040"). This shows you listened.

### 2. The recommended plan (one, maybe two)

Name the plan, the term, the premium paying term and the sum assured. If you show alternatives, limit them to two and explain the trade-off in one sentence each. Use [compare plans](/compare-plans) or [plan comparison](/plan-comparison) to pick the best-fit option before the meeting.

### 3. What you pay

Show the premium clearly:

| Mode | Premium (incl. GST) |
|---|---|
| Yearly | ₹ — |
| Half-yearly | ₹ — |
| Monthly (NACH) | ₹ — |

Get exact figures from the [LIC premium calculator](/premium-calculator), which shows GST for the first year and renewals separately. Mention that yearly mode usually costs less overall.

### 4. What you get

Split this into two clear boxes:

- **Guaranteed:** sum assured, guaranteed additions (if the plan has them), and death benefit.
- **Projected (not guaranteed):** bonuses based on current or past rates.

The [maturity calculator](/maturity-calculator) shows these separately. Saying "bonus guaranteed nahi hai, lekin pichhle saalon mein itna mila hai" ("the bonus isn't guaranteed, but this is what was paid in past years") builds more trust than hiding it.

### 5. Protection for the family

Clients buy LIC for safety. Show the death benefit at a glance: "Agar kal kuch ho jaye, toh family ko kam se kam ₹X milega" ("If something happens tomorrow, your family gets at least ₹X"). If their total cover is too low, suggest adding a term plan. Honesty here earns referrals.

### 6. Tax benefit and next step

Mention the Section 80C deduction (old tax regime) and the conditions for tax-free maturity under Section 10(10D), without promising exact tax savings. Use the [tax calculator](/tax-calculator) for an estimate. Then end with a clear next step: the documents needed and when you'll meet.

### Budget-based presentations: the underrated closer

Many Indian families think in monthly budgets: "Hum ₹3,000 mahina de sakte hain" ("We can afford ₹3,000 a month"). Instead of starting from a plan and asking if they can afford it, start from the budget and show the best combination it buys. The [budget presentation](/budget-presentation) tool does exactly this, and it removes the most common objection, "bahut mehenga hai" ("it's too expensive"), before it comes up.

### Family mix: one proposal, whole household

A family mix presentation covers the earning member, the spouse and children in one document. It shows total premium, total cover and each person's benefits. It is ideal for newly married couples and young parents, and it often results in multiple policies from a single meeting.

## Presenting in Hindi and regional languages

Clients decide faster when they understand every word. Some tips:

- Use simple terms such as "premium" (kist), "maturity par milega" (paid at maturity) and "family ki suraksha" (family protection) instead of jargon like "SRB" and "FAB".
- Read numbers in lakh and crore, not millions.
- Keep a one-line summary at the top in the client's preferred language.
- Explain one key term per meeting, not ten.

## Sharing on WhatsApp

Most presentations are now reviewed on a phone, often by the spouse or a parent who wasn't in the meeting. So:

- Export as a single-page PDF or image that is readable without zooming.
- Put your name, phone number and agency code on it. A [digital business card](/business-card) helps here too.
- Send a short voice note explaining the three key numbers.
- Follow up after 48 hours, not 48 minutes.

## Compliance checklist

- Always show guaranteed and non-guaranteed benefits separately.
- Use official premium and bonus figures, and say when a projection is an estimate.
- Don't compare products misleadingly. Disclose existing policies in the proposal form.
- Follow LIC and IRDAI rules on advertisements and branding.
- Give the client the official Benefit Illustration before they sign the proposal.

### How do I make an LIC policy presentation in Hindi?

Keep the structure the same (goal, plan, premium, benefits, protection, next step) and write the headings and summary in simple Hindi. Explain technical terms like bonus in plain words, and show amounts in lakh.

### Should I show bonus in an LIC presentation?

Yes, but always label it as projected and not guaranteed. Show the guaranteed benefits separately so the client understands the minimum they will receive.

### How many plans should I show a client?

Ideally one recommended plan and at most one alternative. More options usually delay the decision. Use a comparison tool beforehand to choose the best fit for the client's goal and budget.
